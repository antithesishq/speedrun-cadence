## The short version

The results are strong, and now complete in one important way: every failing check in the long runs is explained, as a finding, a known consequence of one, or (plainly labelled) our own test-code error. Ten findings survived adversarial review, eight in the server and two in the Go client. Lead with the signal story: five server findings lose, duplicate or reorder acknowledged signals. Open with [a reset retried by the client is applied a second time](speedrun-report.md#retried-reset-applied-twice) (a regression since #5821, a one-line core fix, now confirmed a second time on unpatched v1.4.5), then [a signal lost when a reset races an in-flight decision](speedrun-report.md#acked-signal-lost-on-reset) (no faults needed), then [signals reordered after a reload from Postgres](speedrun-report.md#buffered-signals-reordered-on-reload) (a missing `ORDER BY`, no faults needed). Two fixes are verified end to end ([shutdown panic](speedrun-report.md#sigterm-during-startup-panics), [signal lost on reset](speedrun-report.md#acked-signal-lost-on-reset) for its own mechanism) and one partly ([signal duplicated after reset](speedrun-report.md#retried-signal-duplicated-after-reset)); the run with the complete patch set was still running. Caveats not to forget: about half of the failing checks in the long runs were our own test-code errors (say so if asked; the report lists them); the "survives a reset" counts mix two findings; and the [continue-as-new duplicate](speedrun-report.md#retried-signal-duplicated-after-continue-as-new) rates overstate production because our workflow continues as new every 3 or 10 signals (or never, drawn per workflow).

**Caveats:**
- The root-cause analysis was blinded procedurally: the model may have seen a known bug in its training data.
- **Mixed counts:** "an acknowledged signal survives a reset of its workflow" counts both [signal lost on reset](speedrun-report.md#acked-signal-lost-on-reset) and [reset applied twice](speedrun-report.md#retried-reset-applied-twice) (on v1.4.5 a timing triage suggests mostly the former; on the fix check all 362 sampled examples of 373 were the latter; the other 11 were not classified). "A signal is applied at most once in a workflow that was reset" mixes three findings. The non-reset signal checks mix the continue-as-new duplicate with test-code errors. Never quote one of these counts as one finding's frequency.
- **Rates that overstate production:** the [continue-as-new duplicate](speedrun-report.md#retried-signal-duplicated-after-continue-as-new) (our counter continues as new every 3 or 10 signals, or never, drawn per workflow; per its adversarial review); the [shutdown panic](speedrun-report.md#sigterm-during-startup-panics) (Antithesis stops nodes during startup far more than production); the [signal reordering](speedrun-report.md#buffered-signals-reordered-on-reload) is made more frequent by our 20-entry history cache, though the failed-decision trigger needs no tuning.
- The fix-check runs are v1.4.5-based. Master has identical code on each finding's path (checked by `diff` of the files and `git apply --check` of the patches on master).
- "Verified" for [signal lost on reset](speedrun-report.md#acked-signal-lost-on-reset) rests on the absence of its mechanism in 362 sampled residual examples, not on the check reaching zero. The running `fc87ca1b` should settle it.
- [Stale history](speedrun-report.md#stale-history-after-shard-steal): the checker asks for more than Cadence documents (no promise of monotonic reads across shard moves). The adversarial review kept it because the read contradicts a completed result 0.6 ms earlier, and it reproduced with no faults at all. The long-run analysis raised the observed window from about 4 s to at least 140 s.
- [Signal reordering](speedrun-report.md#buffered-signals-reordered-on-reload): Cadence does not document signal order explicitly; the review judged that this affects severity, not validity.
- [Go-client stall](speedrun-report.md#retried-decision-heartbeat-stalls-workflow): still a single occurrence; the assertion's 5-minute window is stricter than Cadence's 30-minute backstop. The defect (a dropped local-activity result, a no-progress loop) stands; the stall is bounded.
- [Parent stuck after child cancel](speedrun-report.md#child-cancel-ack-after-close): the adversarial review corrected which server step timed out (the cancel call to the child committed but timed out, not the ack); the outcome is the same, and the report uses the corrected version.

## Unconfirmed findings

<a id="lost-activity-task-after-shard-move"></a>
**A retried activity task is never delivered after shard moves** (report: [unconfirmed](speedrun-report.md#lost-activity-task-after-shard-move)).
- **Seen:** master-b [`c26baba5`][r-c26b] only, workflow chain-57, 7 failing examples of "every started workflow closes once faults stop", one origin (moment input_hash `-7207163401555546203`, vtime `601.42`). The retried attempt was scheduled, its transfer task processed and deleted, but matching neither persisted nor delivered it; recovery would come only at schedule-to-start (1 h). Shard 12 moved twice just before.
- **The doubt:** the task may have been sync-matched to a poller whose `RecordActivityTaskStarted` then failed and was dropped, which Cadence documents-ish as recovered by the schedule-to-start timeout. The data can't separate the two.
- **Tried:** one debugging session at the failing moment ([chain57_exec.txt](../antithesis/scratchbook/logs/hunt-liveness/chain57_exec.txt), [chain57_db.txt](../antithesis/scratchbook/logs/hunt-liveness/chain57_db.txt)). No recurrence in the other four long runs.
- **What would settle it:** a targeted run with matching debug logging (AddActivityTask, sync-match, RecordActivityTaskStarted errors) for the task list, or schedule-to-start at 60 s to see whether recovery happens by timeout; then an adversarial review. [Analysis](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-liveness.md#c5-chain-57-master-b-only-7-examples-one-origin-suspect).

## Method notes

- **False positives caught before the long runs:** about 15, all fixed in the driver or harness (listed in [speedrun-log.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/speedrun-log.md) and [report-facts.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/report-facts.md#false-positives-caught-all-fixed-in-the-driverharness-speedrun-logmd-lists-each)). The most instructive:
  - [a result read from a reset's base run](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/signal-lost-during-reset.md) looked exactly like a lost signal; the driver now pins result reads to the current run and tracks reset bases;
  - [a timed-out, uncommitted reset recorded as a base](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/fixverify-stragglers.md) produced "workflow never closes" stragglers;
  - [orphaned Postgres locks from killed servers](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/shard-unavailable-after-faults.md) held shard rows for hours; fixed with production-like Postgres timeouts, not by excluding Postgres from faults.
- **False positives in the long runs (frozen driver, not fixed yet):** the second edition traced every failing check, and about half were ours. Be ready to say so. In order of how much they inflated the reports:
  - **reused workflow IDs** (the main one): requests sent by ID only (signal, cancel, terminate, finish, start) to an earlier run were still in flight when `RuleReuseClosed` started a new run on the ID; Cadence correctly routed them to the new run. This explains "only applies signals that clients sent" and "returns the result its inputs determine" (identical counts), most non-reset signal-once failures, "ends only as completed …", "second start … rejected" and most of "reuse policy" ([hunt-unknown-signals-result.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-unknown-signals-result.md), [hunt-unexpected-close.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-unexpected-close.md), [hunt-second-start.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-second-start.md), [hunt-reuse-policy.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-reuse-policy.md)); the reuse-policy rule also had a check-then-act race between concurrent workers;
  - **timer check with zero tolerance:** the guarantee allowed for Antithesis clock jitter, the assertion did not ([hunt-timer-early.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-timer-early.md)). The timer analysis did not look at the before-the-fix build, so the first edition's puzzle (0 failures there) is still unexplained; fewer clock skips landing on timer timestamps is a guess, not checked;
  - **"every client is served again":** something other than the fault injector stopped the client containers and then the servers near the end of some timelines (Postgres stayed up in `3e9720ca`, and client1 survived in `c26baba5`; the cause, likely environment teardown, is not confirmed), and `eventually` then probed dead sockets ([hunt-liveness.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-liveness.md#l1-the-whole-sut-is-shut-down-then-eventually-runs-against-dead-containers-harness)). Worth asking Antithesis why the branch is torn down mid-timeline with no fault event;
  - **straggler bookkeeping:** the driver's own terminate of a run left in the reset-base set, `eventually`'s finish beating an in-flight start, and reads landing on a duplicate-start run ([hunt-liveness.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/hunt-liveness.md)).
  Each analysis has a proposed driver fix; none is applied, so a re-run would need them first.
- **Harness gap:** the history checker at first did not record which timer a `TimerCanceled` refers to, so the known bug was undetectable ([history-missing-close-event.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/history-missing-close-event.md#harness-gap-the-known-bug-signature-8595-is-not-detectable)); fixed before the known-bug run that reproduced it. The 1-minute `decisionHeartbeatTimeout` hid the Go-client stall in the long runs ([heartbeat-stall-2nd.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/heartbeat-stall-2nd.md)); hence `37b22144`.
- **Iteration runs to reach green:** 7 on master (5 that ran for more than a minute), 6 on v1.4.5; each was cancelled once its false positives were understood. The long-run driver was then frozen.
- **Fault types used:** Antithesis `high_availability` (kill, stop, pause, partitions, degraded links, CPU throttling, clock jitter) on everything but the driver, in every run.
- **Fault types needed but unavailable:** none identified. Cross-cluster replication was not exercised (see XDC below).
- **Recurring warnings:** the client containers' exit code 2 in every long run (master 19 / 22). Not triaged further; it may be the same end-of-timeline shutdown as above, but that was not checked.
- **Counts for [`3e9720ca`][r-3e97]** are now in [props-3e9720ca.txt](../antithesis/scratchbook/runs/props-3e9720ca.txt) (the first edition lacked them).
- **XDC:** a two-cluster replication harness was built and validated locally on both versions ([antithesis-xdc/README.md](../antithesis-xdc/README.md)) but not run in Antithesis before the deadline.
- **Clock note:** speedrun-log timestamps after 19:30Z on 2026-11-18 ran about 30 minutes ahead of real UTC; the run table uses Antithesis usage times.
- **Document review:** a fresh-reviewer truth check found 15 problems; all were corrected in the report and this briefing. Notes on that pass:
  - run start times now follow [runs.tsv](../antithesis/scratchbook/runs.tsv) (15:56:23Z, 16:01:55Z); [report-facts.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/report-facts.md) still says 15:57Z / 16:03Z and was not edited;
  - three example moments in the report (input_hash `4569408548377247411`, `3180257910619170844`, `5493115663274640094`) had no source in any local file and were removed;
  - the report's image digests were replaced by the ones recorded in the runs' Antithesis container logs (the earlier ones were per-platform manifest digests from the build logs, or had no source).

## Content hooks

- "One API call, two resets": a retried `ResetWorkflowExecution` is committed twice because its dedup check has been dead code since a 2024 change; the fix is one line.
- "Acknowledged, then gone, with no faults": a signal acknowledged 8 ms before a reset finished was written into the run being terminated, and never seen by any run after it.
- "A missing ORDER BY": signals acknowledged in order come back from Postgres in disk order after a reload; Cadence's own code comment already notes the symptom for activity events.
- "Completed, then running again 0.6 ms later", and for at least 140 s: a paused history host resumes and keeps serving a finished workflow as running.
- Candidate diagram: the buffered-signal timeline (signal buffered during a decision → reset reads next event ID → termination flushes the buffer → reapply misses it), from [signal-lost-at-reset-master.md](../antithesis/scratchbook/rca/signal-lost-at-reset-master.md#sequence-utc-2032xx-event-timestamps-and-task-ids-from-the-json-histories).
- Angle for testing method: the known-bug check found a gap in our own checker before it found the bug; and the honest triage of every long-run failure, including our own mistakes, is what lets the ten findings stand.
